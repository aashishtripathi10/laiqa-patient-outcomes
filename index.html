Laiqa Wellness · Patient Outcomes Report

Patient outcomes from the Laiqa hormonal-health programme.

A consolidated read of the Laiqa hormonal-health programme: who we saw, how we measure and personalise care, and the outcomes produced — across the full patient population, paid and unpaid alike. The thesis is protocol quality, not payment status.

Data window: Jun 2024 → Jun 2026 Compiled: 9 Jun 2026
What this report argues, and on what evidence. Laiqa's protocol is rigorous (a measured, personalised, clinically-governed engine — Sections 03–04), evidence-grounded (Section 09), and produces real outcomes (Sections 05–08) across symptoms, objective cycle data, and validated questionnaires. Because protocol quality is independent of who paid, the evidence population is everyone the programme treated — free-trial, unpaid, and paid — with each statistic labelled by its own denominator and method.
Evidence discipline (read once). This describes the pre-restructure (7 Apr 2026) clinician-led programme plus the May-2026 WhatsApp relaunch where noted. It is real-world clinical-operations data, not an RCT: no control arm, no blinding, no pre-registration. Three integrity rules are enforced throughout: (1) outcome rates are computed from clinician-documented symptom resolution — any rate tied to a diagnostic label rather than an observed symptom is excluded; (2) thyroid biomarker deltas are co-medication-confounded (levothyroxine concurrent in the cohort) and are shown illustratively, never as protocol-attributable; (3) outcome rates are shown per-completer and intention-to-treat where the completion denominator is known. Statistics tagged objective are computed from device/lab data; self-report from clinician notes or questionnaires.
How independent is this evidence? This is a clinical case series from our earlier clinician-led programme, not a trial: no control arm, no blinding, no pre-registration. Outcomes were recorded at the month-1 clinic check-in by the treating clinicians — the team that delivered the protocols also marked the outcomes — so please read the rates as our own clinical documentation, not independent adjudication. Closing that independence gap is exactly what the forward clinical programme is being built for.

A payment-agnostic evidence population

Free-trial and unpaid users receive the same first-line protocol (seed cycling, induction agents, detox, Kanchanar): the frequency of these prescriptions in clinician notes is comparable across paid and unpaid users. The documented difference is follow-up duration and consultation cadence, not the first-line protocol itself. So an unpaid user's improvement is valid evidence of protocol quality — with the honest caveat that the parity evidence is prescription-frequency, not a full plan-depth audit.

03How we measured — the evidence instruments

The historical outcomes in this report came from four distinct measurement instruments, each with a different evidence quality. Understanding which instrument backs which claim is essential to reading the numbers correctly.

Objective App-tracked cycle dates

Period start/end dates logged directly in the app by 763 users. Cycle-length standard deviation computed from production period_cycles table — no self-report, no clinician interpretation. The most defensible signal in this report. Used in §05.

Objective Before/after lab panels

HbA1c, prolactin, AMH, SGOT/SGPT, vitamin D, uric acid, USG imaging — drawn from actual lab reports attached to patient consultations. Curated from ~29 cases; small, cherry-picked, no control arm. Used in §07.

Self-report Clinician symptom notes

Doctors marked which concerns were present or resolved at each follow-up visit. The backbone of the n=180 symptom-resolution data (§06). Subject to logging-mode variation (status-list vs narrative) and the logging-artefact caveat in §06.

Self-report Validated questionnaires — B2B only

MWQ (Menstrual Wellness Questionnaire) and LQ (Lifestyle Quotient) — internal Laiqa self-report questionnaires administered before/after to Deloitte and Advatix cohorts. They embed some established sub-scales (Perceived Stress Scale, SATED sleep), but the composites are not externally validated or published — treat as consistent self-report deltas, not published-instrument scores.

The PMOS Health Score — a clinician-designed 0–100 weighted composite across 6 dimensions (symptoms 20%, weight 20%, sleep/stress/cycle/lifestyle 15% each) — is the next-generation outcome instrument, methodology signed off by Dr. Nivedita. It did not measure the historical cohort in this report. Source: wiki/clinical/health-score.md, wiki/clinical/measurement-matrix.md.

04How we personalise — the driver engine

The protocol's defining feature is that it treats the driver, not the disease label. One engine collapses PMOS, hypothyroid, endometriosis, amenorrhea, NAFLD and IBS into five root drivers, then sequences a personalised plan with documented decision rules, safety gates and conflict resolution. This is the single strongest body of protocol-quality evidence in the knowledge base.

5 root drivers

Insulin · Cortisol · Inflammatory/Androgen · Gut · Thyroid. Signal-weighted scoring (e.g. sugar cravings +3 insulin, abnormal TSH +3 thyroid). 1 primary + 1 secondary per user; primary confirmed by a 2-of-3 signal cluster (false-positive guard); labs override symptoms (fasting insulin ≥15 → insulin-primary).

Disciplined timeline

Observe-only week 1 · earliest layering day 14 · primary-driver change only at day 28 and only on high-compliance + zero-improvement. Compliance zones: ≥80% clear / 50–79% clinician judgement / <50% simplify. "A plan 60% followed is not a failed plan — it's an untested one."

Conflict resolution

All 10 driver pairs have documented conflicts + week-by-week sequencing (insulin+thyroid: carb-restrict vs T3-suppress → 40–42% carb). 4-level tiebreaker: safety → most-suffering → most-recent-change → least-restrictive. Second axis: dosha decides food form, driver decides food selection.

Care is multi-modal and safety-gated end to end: a red-flag screen at intake (TSH >50, myxoedema flags), per-instance CDP clinician approval for medicines/supplements, and pre-specified healing arcs that set outcome expectations honestly — for stress-driven amenorrhea, "period regulation is the last thing to return: expect 8–12 weeks, not 2." Outcome timelines are clinical, not marketing.

Source: wiki/clinical/ — drivers.md, driver-change-rules.md, driver-combinations.md, decision-tree.md, dosha-overlay.md, diet-protocol.md; condition packs add a validated 25-item Perimenopause Assessment Scale + STRAW+10 staging (conditions/perimenopause.md) and subtype→driver routing for hypothyroid/amenorrhea. The measurement layer for this engine — the PMOS Health Score and its signal source-of-truth matrix — is clinician-designed and ready for build; see §03.

05Outcomes — objective signal objective

The one outcome computed from the production database rather than clinician notes: app-tracked menstrual cycle dates. Regularity = cycle-length standard deviation under 5 days, the programme's own pre-specified definition of "treatment working." Every figure below is reproducible from a query saved alongside this report — no number here is unverifiable.

Paired within-user test (early vs late cycles, n=23): SD improved in 11/23 = 48% — indistinguishable from chance. Causation not established.
Read this honestly — it is the most rigorous thing in the report. Roughly 41–53% of users with enough logged cycles have regular cycle length (the range spans the ≥3 vs ≥4-cycle thresholds). This is a current-state descriptive statistic: it says these women have regular cycles, not that Laiqa regularised them. We ran the harder causal test — does each woman's cycle variability fall from her early cycles to her later ones? — and on the n=23 with enough data it does not yet show a signal (48%, a coin-flip). We are reporting this rather than a more flattering number on purpose: an earlier internal "64%" figure could not be reproduced from the current data under any standard method (the ceiling across eight definitions is 57.8%), so it has been retired. The defensible objective claim today is "a plausible share of trackable users have regular cycles; the protocol's causal effect on regularity is not yet proven and needs the larger post-launch cohort." Cycle regularity is the right endpoint — we just don't yet have the controlled data to claim causation on it.

Source: prod period_cycles, re-run transparently 2026-06-10; full SQL saved at outputs/cycle-regularity-queries.sql + reconciliation across 8 metric definitions at outputs/cycle-regularity-reconcile.sql. Method: cycle length = gap between consecutive period_start dates, filtered to 10–120d (excludes double-logged spotting and tracking breaks), sample standard deviation. The retired "64% of 39" (2026-06-06) had no saved query and no reconstructable cohort.

Thyroid deltas are shown illustratively only. All thyroid biomarker movement in this cohort is co-medication-confounded: the standard-of-care in this programme is to layer Ayurvedic support alongside the patient's existing levothyroxine, and in cases where labs don't improve, the clinical team recommends adding allopathic thyroid medication. The same discipline applies to the metabolic rows above (HbA1c, liver enzymes, lipids): no concurrent metformin or anti-diabetic medication is documented for those cases, but it is not excluded either — read all biomarker deltas as case evidence, not protocol-attributable.

Three honest caveats. (0) Concentration: Deloitte is ~85% of the B2B book (1,863 of 2,201 records; ~42% of the entire dataset) — the B2B signal leans heavily on one employer at a 1–2 month checkpoint. (1) The Deloitte MWQ migration is computed on 38 of 150 (~25%) who filled progress data — the same completer-denominator discipline as B2C applies. (2) B2B outcomes exist only at the 1–2 month checkpoint for Deloitte (no 3m/6m follow-up); the Advatix MWQ was measured at month 4. The two cohorts used different clinician-logging modes and should not be compared head-to-head on outcome rates. (3) The Advatix −7.5 BMI figure (32.5→25.0) is exceptional — roughly 20 kg of weight loss for an average-height user — and is computed on the overweight/obese users of the n=24 cohort, a subset that also skews to consistently-engaged users. The MWQ 67→87% is likewise among consistently engaged users*, not all 24. Treat as upper-bound signals, not typical-user expectations. The genuine finding is the completion contrast: ~72–94% (employer-paid) vs ~50% (B2C self-paid). Read alongside the B2C drop-off data (§10: dissatisfaction with results just 4.6%), this points directionally to drop-off being driven by who-pays rather than whether-it-works — but the two cohorts are not like-for-like, so treat this as a strong hypothesis, not a proven decoupling.

09The evidence basis — is the protocol grounded?

Protocol quality also means the interventions are derived from evidence, not invented. They are — with named citations and a defined evidence-grading model — though with one honest boundary about what is shipped today.

Protocols trace to peer-reviewed sources

  • The macronutrient-cycling and seed-cycling protocols cite Varady & Hellerstein 2007, Harvie & Howell 2017, Paoli 2012, Paddon-Jones 2008 (AJCN reviews).
  • Myo-inositol carries Tier-1 PCOS evidence; used in the womb-healing protocol.
  • CBT for amenorrhea RCT: 87.5% resumed ovarian activity vs 25% observation-only — backs the mind-body/stress arm.
  • The stress×emotional-eating×sleep mechanism (cortisol/insulin drivers) is grounded in Dweck et al. 2014, Appetite (3-way interaction F=5.52, p=.022).

A defined evidence-grading model

A T1–T5 tiering system (meta-analysis → traditional-consensus), two-gate clinician approval per claim, and a Tier-0 layer of internal clinician-confirmed Laiqa case evidence (highest population-fit for Indian women).

Current state: today's recommendations are clinician-authored and grounded in the sources cited above and our internal Tier-0 case dataset.

Why people leave — and why they stay

Churn is logistics & finance, not dissatisfaction. Coded drop-off (n=256): compliance 39%, financial 23%, "managing on her own" 20% — dissatisfaction with results just 4.6%.

Renewal is driven by results. Of coded renewal reasons (n=21), ~67% cited "got results / health improved" (small n — only 21 of ~1,045 rows were reason-coded). We acquire on weight, but we retain on felt chronic-symptom relief.

11Safety, non-responders & honest limitations

An outcomes report that only counts wins is not credible. The same sources record harm signals, non-responders, and structural ceilings — disclosing them is what makes everything above believable.

Safety / adverse signals on record

  • Prolonged bleeding — a churned user reported "3 months continuously" after the protocol; an induction agent (Rajpravartani Vati) triggered a 10-day bleed in another case → diagnostic workup.
  • Symptom worsening — one user logged "stress — no improvement, in fact increased."
  • Mid-plan hospitalisation (dengue — not protocol-caused, forced a pause).
  • Procedure risk — Virechana (therapeutic purgation) + herbs taken alongside levothyroxine/metformin/OCPs; interaction/over-purgation risk not systematically tracked.

Structural limitations

  • No systematic adverse-event register — harm surfaces only incidentally; the true rate is unknown and almost certainly under-counted.
  • No control / comparator arm — improvements cannot be causally attributed to the protocol; cycle/skin conditions also regress naturally.
  • Cherry-picked biomarkers, co-medication confounds, non-standardised imaging.
  • All counts use the latest DB-verified figures: 947 strict paying patients, ~2,900 consultations, 5 doctors across the full programme period.
The defensible bottom line. Laiqa ran a rigorous, personalised, clinically-governed protocol on ~1,400 women across the hormonal-metabolic spectrum — analysed across symptom check-ins, app-tracked cycles, employer-cohort questionnaires and paired labs, and grounded in named evidence. It produced self-reported symptom resolution of 84–87% for top clusters among those followed up (≈72–74% intention-to-treat on the 85.2% follow-up denominator), validated-questionnaire gains in two employer cohorts, and curated biomarker/imaging reversals — with churn driven by logistics not dissatisfaction (4.6%) and renewal driven by results (~67%). On the objective cycle data, ~41–53% of trackable users have regular cycles today, but a within-user causal test does not yet show the protocol drives regularisation (§05) — we report that plainly rather than over-claim. With the outcome marker being our own clinical team, this is a case series, not an efficacy claim — what it is not yet is a controlled, registered trial that can prove causation, with a complete safety denominator. The asset is a rare real-world dataset and a disciplined protocol.
